Dark matter outweighs everything we can see by a factor of five. We don't yet know what it is. Our group works to find out — through direct-detection experiments, novel sensor technologies, and the theoretical modelling that lets us interpret what we observe.

About

Dark Matter Manchester is a research group within the Particle Physics theme at the University of Manchester. We bring together experimentalists and phenomenologists to address one of the most conspicuous open questions in physics: what is the matter that shapes galaxies and the cosmic web, yet emits no light?

Our programme is broad. We contribute to the DarkSide collaboration and to the construction and physics output of DarkSide-20k — a multi-tonne argon-based detector under construction at Gran Sasso. We develop levitating-sensor technology that could extend the reach of direct searches into mass regimes inaccessible to conventional detectors. And we pursue the theoretical and phenomenological work that connects underlying particle-physics models to what experiments actually see, across different detector targets.

Research

Direct detection · DarkSide

We contribute to DarkSide-20k and the reinterpretation of DarkSide-50 data, mapping the experiment's sensitivity to spin-independent, spin-dependent, and effective-field-theory motivated dark matter models — including channels accessible only through forced companion operators.

Levitating sensors

Optically and magnetically levitated mesoscopic sensors offer an emerging route to ultra-low-threshold detection. We develop the sensitivity projections, signal modelling, and physics-case studies that motivate this new class of experiment.

Phenomenology & theory

From non-relativistic effective field theory to UV-complete mediator models, our theoretical work bridges fundamental physics and detector observables, including the target dependence of signals across argon, xenon, germanium and beyond.

Neutrinos & CEνNS

Dual-target direct-detection experiments offer a unique window on coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering and beyond- Standard-Model neutrino physics, complementing the dark matter programme.
