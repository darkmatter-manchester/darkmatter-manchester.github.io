<h2 class="section-label">Overview</h2>

Direct-detection experiments measure energies and rates; turning those
measurements into statements about dark matter requires a theoretical
framework that connects fundamental particle models to what a given
detector actually sees. This is the work of dark matter phenomenology.

Our phenomenology spans non-relativistic effective field theory — a
largely model-independent description of dark matter–nucleon interactions
— through to UV-complete mediator models with definite high-energy
completions. A recurring theme is the *target dependence* of signals: how
the same underlying physics imprints differently on argon, xenon,
germanium and other detector materials, and how combining targets sharpens
what can be inferred.

<h2 class="section-label">Current activities</h2>

Current work includes mapping experimental sensitivity across the space of
effective dark matter–nucleon operators, including interactions accessible
only through forced companion operators; reinterpreting published
experimental results under broader model assumptions; and cross-target
comparisons that inform the design of future experiments.
