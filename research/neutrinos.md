<h2 class="section-label">Overview</h2>

Neutrinos are both a signal and a background for dark matter experiments,
and a rich subject in their own right. Coherent elastic neutrino–nucleus
scattering (CEvNS) produces nuclear recoils almost identical to those
expected from dark matter — the so-called "neutrino fog" — and dual-target
detectors offer a unique window onto it and onto beyond-Standard-Model
neutrino physics.

The group's neutrino programme spans CEvNS signals in direct-detection
experiments, searches for new physics in the neutrino sector, and the
prospects for observing neutrinoless double beta decay in xenon-136 — a
process that, if seen, would establish the neutrino as its own antiparticle.

<h2 class="section-label">Current activities</h2>

Current activities include sensitivity studies for low-energy solar CEvNS
and beyond-Standard-Model anomalies in low-threshold liquid-argon
detectors, and studies of neutrinoless double beta decay prospects.
